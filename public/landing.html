How it works

From sign-in to a resolved repair

The normal day-to-day flow for keeping boards running, in the order you will actually use it. Renting a board follows a different path — that is covered in Booking.

01

Sign in to your account

Use the username and password issued to you. What you see afterwards depends on your role — the system loads only the modules you are allowed to use.

Login screenAny role
02

Find the board you care about

Open Devices and search by device ID, client, serial number or room. You get its live status, OS version, OPS module and last-seen time.

DevicesAny role
03

Raise a ticket when something breaks

Open a ticket against the affected board, describe the fault and set a priority. Customers can do this from their own portal.

TicketsAny role
04

Track the repair to completion

Technicians pick up assigned jobs and move them through Received → Diagnosing → Repairing → Resolved. Parts come straight from Inventory.

TicketsInventoryAdmin · Technician
05

Keep an eye on the bigger picture

Use the dashboard and reports to spot repeat offenders, watch stock, and check whether a board is still under warranty.

Booking

How booking a board works

Renting a board runs on its own track, separate from maintenance. This is what happens between requesting a unit and getting it back on the shelf — including who can do what, and how the price is worked out.

01

Request a board for a date range

Open Rentals and create a booking: choose the board, the start and end date, a purpose, the delivery address and a contact. A customer can also start from their own portal with Book a Device.

RentalsAdmin · Customer
02

The system blocks double-bookings

Before anything is saved, the board is checked against every other booking that is still Pending, Approved or Active. If the dates overlap, the request is refused and the message names the booking that already holds the unit.

Conflict checkAutomatic
03

A new booking starts as Pending

Creating a booking is a request, not a confirmed reservation. It sits in the queue until an administrator reviews it and sets the daily rate and deposit for that rental.

PendingAwaiting approval
04

Approved, then handed over

Approving locks the agreed price in. When the board physically leaves the building an administrator activates the booking: the status becomes Active and the deposit is recorded as collected.

ApprovedActiveAdmin
05

While the board is out

The unit is shown as rented out on its device record. If the return date passes without a hand-back, the booking can be marked Overdue so it stands out on the dashboard and in the rentals list.

ActiveOverdueAdmin
06

Returned, and released again

Marking the booking Returned stamps the actual return date and frees the board for the next request. A booking cancelled before it starts releases the dates straight away.

ReturnedCancelledAdmin
Status flow Pending → Approved → Active → Returned off-path Overdue Cancelled

Who can book

Administrators can book any board on behalf of any customer. A customer can book only for their own organisation. Technicians have no booking access at all — Rentals is not in their sidebar.

Admin · Customer

How the price is worked out

Total cost is the daily rate multiplied by the number of days, and both the start and end date count — so Monday to Friday is five days. A deposit is recorded on top, set per booking by an administrator.

Admin

Bookings stay private

A booking belongs to the account that created it, not to the company. Two logins under the same customer will not see each other's bookings, and a customer never sees another organisation's.

About

Built around the Millennium SmartBoard

Millennium is a dynamic interactive touch panel used for presentations in classrooms, conference rooms and home theatres — anywhere that calls for collaborative, engaging content. This management system is the operations layer behind those boards.

It was built by Brains Infinite Innovations Inc. so that schools, offices and service teams could stop tracking boards in spreadsheets. Instead, every unit has a permanent record: where it is, who owns it, what has been replaced on it, whether it is still under warranty, and when it is due for attention.

  • One shared record for every board across all your sites.
  • Role-based access — people see only what their job needs.
  • Faults caught early, so fewer lessons and meetings are interrupted.
  • Cloud-hosted, so it works from a phone in the corridor or a desk.
